# Quick Access & History

As your notebooks grow, the notes you actually work with day to day are a small, shifting set. VNote gives you two fast ways back to them: **Quick Access** for the notes you deliberately keep close, and **History** for the notes you have recently opened.

## Quick Access

Quick Access is a small, pinned list of notes you want one keystroke away — a daily journal, a running to-do note, a project's index page. Unlike history, it does not change unless you change it.

- **Add a note** to Quick Access from its context menu or the Quick Access menu.
- **Open Quick Access** from the toolbar or its shortcut to jump straight to a pinned note.
- **Remove** a note when it is no longer front-of-mind.

Because Quick Access holds only what you choose, it stays a short, high-signal list of your current focus.

## History

History records the notes you open, automatically. When you need to get back to something you were reading an hour ago without remembering where it lives, the **History** dock widget lists your recently opened notes in order. Select any entry to reopen it.

History is maintained for you — there is nothing to curate. It complements [Search](../search/index.md) and [United Entry](../search/united-entry.md): search finds notes by what's in them, while history finds notes by what you were just doing.

## When to use which

- Reach for **Quick Access** for notes you return to over and over — pin them once and forget the path.
- Reach for **History** to retrace your steps to something you touched recently.
- Reach for [Search](../search/index.md) or [Tags](tags.md) when you need to find a note by its content or topic rather than your recent activity.

Together these keep navigation fast even in large, deeply nested notebooks.
